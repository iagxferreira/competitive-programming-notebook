Toolchain and I/O

Zero algorithmic content. The point is to make the template, `Locale.US`, and fast reading automatic before anything hard depends on them. If you fumble I/O later you will not know whether your algorithm or your reader is wrong.

Binary search

First real primitive, and the one you will reuse most. Own lower-bound binary search outright - not `Arrays.binarySearch`, the loop. Get the boundary convention and the overflow-safe midpoint into muscle memory here and they stay correct forever.

0/3

  • recursive, with the overflow-prone (lo+hi)/2 midpoint

  • linear scan where O(log n) was explicitly required

3

Two pointers

The second primitive. Every one of these rests on the same exchange argument: moving a pointer discards only candidates that provably cannot be the answer. If you cannot state why a move is safe, you have memorised it rather than understood it.

0/8

  • allocated a second array; the O(1) triple-reversal is the point

Using the Review line

Every card ends with last: never   confidence: 0/5. Set the date and a confidence when you solve it. Revisit anything at 3 or below after a week, anything at 1 or 2 after two days. That is the whole system — the cards exist so a revisit costs two minutes instead of twenty.

A problem you re-solve cold is worth more than three you read the solution to.

One caveat

This archive is interview-style DSA, not competitive programming. The two share fundamentals and diverge after that — contests want speed under pressure, and topics barely represented here. There is not a single graph traversal in these 117 problems.

So run a second track in parallel: Codeforces Div. 2 A and B, on the clock, starting now rather than after Phase 14. This repository builds the foundation. Contests build the thing you actually said you wanted.
